# Brief for intended-purpose and legal review

Status: classification not determined. This document provides facts for a qualified UK medical-device/data-protection adviser; it is not a declaration that Nivvi is exempt or approved.

Nivvi displays heart rate and, for supported protocols, oxygen saturation from external Bluetooth hardware. Adults may enter a child's profile. It stores history, evaluates configurable high/low HR thresholds, warns about repeated/missing readings and provides local alerts. A custom adapter uses inferred packet fields with limited paired observations. The manual sleep feature has been removed. No clinical diagnosis, validated automatic sleep inference or emergency response service is implemented.

Assess the intended population, environment, reliance on alarms and foreseeable use, including the user's stated concern about SVT. Intended purpose must reflect the real functionality, instructions and marketing. Renaming the app “generic Bluetooth” or adding a “not a medical device” sentence does not settle classification. The in-app categorical exemption wording has been removed.

Questions for the assessment:

1. Does this combination of child monitoring, physiological thresholds and alerts fall within medical-device software rules in each launch market? What classification and evidence follow?
2. Can an initial limited-purpose release lawfully ship, and what functionality/claims must change? Do not simply change wording while retaining a conflicting purpose.
3. What validation is required for each sensor protocol, status field, oxygen value, stale-data heuristic and alarm delivery mode?
4. What quality, risk, incident handling, cybersecurity, technical documentation and post-market duties apply?
5. What controller roles, lawful bases, children's-data assessment, DPIA, support retention and privacy rights processes are needed?
6. Review consumer terms, trade name/asset licences and compatibility statements; preserve mandatory consumer rights.

Reference: [MHRA medical-device software guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/medical-devices-software-applications-apps). Keep the written assessment with the release record. Do not advertise SVT detection, life-saving alerts, guaranteed safety, medical-grade oxygen or reliable Silent/Focus bypass without the necessary evidence and authorisation.
